How Much Should a Small Business Spend on Google Ads?

Google AdsBy AI Dizital Marketing16 Sep 20263 min read

“How much should I spend on Google Ads?” There is no single right number, but there is a right way to calculate it. Instead of guessing, work backwards from what a customer is worth to your business.

Step 1: Know What a Customer Is Worth

Start with the average profit you earn from one new customer — including repeat purchases if they usually come back. This tells you the maximum you can afford to spend to acquire one customer while staying profitable.

Step 2: Estimate Your Conversion Rates

You need two numbers:

  • Website conversion rate: the percentage of ad clicks that become enquiries.
  • Close rate: the percentage of enquiries that become paying customers.

If you don’t know these yet, run a small test campaign for a few weeks to find out.

Step 3: Calculate Your Budget

Here is an illustrative example. Suppose a new customer brings ₹10,000 profit, 1 in 4 enquiries becomes a customer, and you want to keep acquisition cost under ₹4,000. That means you can afford up to ₹1,000 per enquiry. If you want 20 customers a month, you need about 80 enquiries — roughly ₹80,000 per month at that cost per lead.

Plug in your own numbers. The formula is simple: monthly budget = enquiries needed × affordable cost per enquiry.

Where Small Businesses Waste Ad Budget

  • Broad match keywords without negatives — your ads show for irrelevant searches.
  • No conversion tracking — you can’t tell which keywords actually bring leads.
  • Sending traffic to the homepage instead of a focused landing page.
  • Targeting all of India when you only serve one city.
  • Running ads 24/7 when nobody is available to answer calls.

How to Start With a Smaller Budget

If your budget is limited, focus it. Choose your single most profitable service, target only high-intent keywords (like “hire”, “near me”, “price”), limit locations, and schedule ads for business hours. A small, focused campaign beats a large, unfocused one.

Remember: Your budget should grow with results. Once a campaign shows a profitable cost per customer, scale it gradually.

Conclusion

A Google Ads budget isn’t an expense to minimise — it’s an investment to measure. Track every lead, calculate your true cost per customer, and scale what works. Need help setting it up properly? See our Google Ads management.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good starting budget for Google Ads?

Start with an amount you can sustain for at least 4–6 weeks so the campaign collects enough data to optimise.

How quickly do Google Ads bring leads?

Well-built campaigns can generate enquiries within days of launch, with performance improving over the first month.

Do I need a landing page for Google Ads?

A dedicated landing page usually converts much better than a homepage because it focuses on one offer and one action.
