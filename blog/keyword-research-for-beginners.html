Keyword Research for Beginners: How to Find the Words Your Customers Search

SEOBy AI Dizital Marketing4 Oct 20263 min read

Every SEO plan starts with one question: what are your customers typing into Google? Keyword research answers it. Get it right and every page you create has a clear purpose. Get it wrong and you rank for words nobody searches — or nobody buys from.

1. Start With Seed Keywords

Seed keywords are the basic terms that describe what you sell. Write down your services, products and the problems you solve, in your customers’ words, not industry jargon. A dental clinic might start with “dentist”, “teeth cleaning”, “root canal” and “braces”.

2. Expand Your List With Free Tools

  • Google autocomplete: type a seed keyword and note the suggestions.
  • People also ask and related searches at the bottom of results pages.
  • Google Search Console: shows the searches your site already appears for — a goldmine for existing websites.
  • Google Keyword Planner: free with a Google Ads account, gives search volume ranges.
  • Google Trends: shows seasonality and compares terms over time.

3. Understand Search Intent

The same topic can be searched for different reasons. Group keywords by what the searcher wants:

  • Informational: “how to whiten teeth at home” — they want to learn. Best served by blog posts and guides.
  • Commercial: “best dentist in Saket” — they are comparing options. Best served by service pages, reviews and comparisons.
  • Transactional: “book dental appointment near me” — they are ready to act. Best served by service and booking pages.
  • Navigational: a brand name — they want a specific website.

Search the keyword yourself and look at the top results. Google is showing you what kind of page it thinks fits that intent.

4. Choose Keywords You Can Win

High-volume keywords are tempting, but they are usually dominated by big websites. A newer or smaller site should focus on:

  • Long-tail keywords — longer, more specific phrases like “invisible braces cost in Delhi”. Fewer searches, but much easier to rank for and often more ready to buy.
  • Local keywords that include your city or area.
  • Keywords where the top results are weak — thin, outdated or not quite matching the question.
Rule of thumb: a keyword with a small search volume that brings ready-to-buy customers is worth more than a huge keyword that only brings browsers.

5. Map Keywords to Pages

Give every important keyword group one home on your site. One page should target one main topic, with closely related keywords supporting it. This avoids two of your pages competing for the same search and makes your internal linking clear.

6. Track and Refine

Once pages are live, watch Google Search Console every month. Look for keywords where you sit on page two — small improvements to those pages often bring quick gains. Keyword research is not a one-time task; customer language and competition keep changing.

Conclusion

Good keyword research means understanding your customers, not just collecting search volumes. Start with their words, respect their intent and pick battles you can win. Next, use our on-page SEO checklist to optimise each page — or ask our SEO team to build your keyword plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Google Keyword Planner free?

Yes. It is free to use with a Google Ads account, and you don’t need to run ads to access keyword ideas.

How many keywords should one page target?

One main keyword or topic, supported by a few closely related variations and questions.

Are long-tail keywords worth targeting?

Yes. They are easier to rank for and often bring visitors who know exactly what they want, so they convert well.
