Google Search Console for Beginners: Set It Up and Use It in 30 Minutes

SEOBy AI Dizital Marketing9 Oct 20263 min read

Google Search Console is a free tool from Google that shows how your website appears in search: which searches you show up for, how many people click, which pages are indexed and what problems Google finds. If you care about SEO, it is the first tool to set up.

1. Choose the Right Property Type

When you add your website, Search Console offers two options:

  • Domain property (recommended): covers every version of your site, including http, https, www and subdomains. It is verified with a DNS record.
  • URL-prefix property: covers only the exact address you enter, such as https://example.com. It can be verified with an HTML file, a meta tag or Google Analytics.

2. Verify Your Website

For a Domain property, Search Console gives you a TXT record that starts with google-site-verification=. Add it in your domain or hosting DNS settings:

  • Type: TXT
  • Name / Host: @
  • Value: the full verification code

Save the record, wait a little while for DNS to update and click Verify. If it fails, wait an hour and try again.

Careful: Add a new record for verification. Do not edit or delete existing A, CNAME or MX records, because they keep your website and email working.

3. Submit Your Sitemap

Open Sitemaps, enter sitemap.xml (or your sitemap’s address) and click Submit. A sitemap lists your important pages so Google can find them faster. The status should change to “Success” after Google reads it.

4. Read the Performance Report

This report shows how your site performs in Google Search. The four main numbers are:

  • Clicks: how many times people clicked through to your site.
  • Impressions: how many times your pages appeared in results.
  • CTR: clicks divided by impressions.
  • Average position: where your pages usually appear.

Switch to the Queries and Pages tabs to see which searches and pages bring traffic. New sites may need a few days before data appears.

5. Check Which Pages Are Indexed

The Pages report under Indexing shows which pages Google has indexed and why others are not. Some exclusions are normal, such as redirects or pages marked noindex. Look closely at important pages listed as “Crawled – currently not indexed” or “Discovered – currently not indexed”, which often need better content or more internal links.

6. Use URL Inspection

Paste any page address into the search bar at the top to see whether it is indexed, when Google last crawled it and which canonical URL Google chose. After publishing or updating an important page, you can click Request indexing.

7. Check Experience Reports

Core Web Vitals and HTTPS reports show whether real visitors get a fast, secure experience. If pages are marked “Poor”, see our guide to website speed and SEO.

8. A Simple Monthly Routine

  1. Compare clicks and impressions with the previous month.
  2. Find queries with many impressions but a low CTR, then improve that page’s title and description.
  3. Find queries where you rank around positions 8–20 and strengthen that page’s content.
  4. Check the Pages report for new errors.
  5. Review any messages from Google.

Conclusion

Search Console turns SEO from guesswork into decisions based on Google’s own data. Set it up once, check it every month and fix what it shows. Want help reading the data and acting on it? See our SEO services in Delhi or the on-page SEO checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Google Search Console free?

Yes. Google Search Console is completely free for any website owner.

How long does Search Console take to show data?

Verification can work within minutes once DNS updates, but performance data for a new property usually takes a few days to appear.

Does submitting a sitemap guarantee indexing?

No. A sitemap helps Google discover your pages, but Google decides what to index based on quality and other signals.
